Project story

Designing a health app that feels calm instead of clinical

The concept began with a simple question: how can a health app support daily habits without making the user feel watched, pressured, or buried in data? The answer was a softer visual system, clearer priorities, and smaller decision points throughout the experience.

Rather than pushing every feature onto one dashboard, I designed the flow around three moments that matter most: checking in, tracking progress, and reflecting on weekly patterns. That structure helped the product feel more supportive and much easier to navigate.

Deliverables

What this case study includes

  • User flow mapping for onboarding, reminders, and daily tracking
  • Low and high fidelity mobile wireframes for core screens
  • A reusable component kit for cards, tags, buttons, and charts
  • Prototype notes focused on clarity, trust, and visual pacing
Process

How the product came together

Each phase narrowed the experience down to what users actually need when they open the app: quick feedback, reassurance, and a clear next action.

01

Research framing

I mapped the emotional highs and lows of health-tracking habits to identify where reminders, progress views, and summaries should feel lighter and more encouraging.

02

Workflow design

The interface was organized into fewer, stronger moments: one glance for today's status, one tap for logging, and one place to reflect on personal trends.

03

Prototype polish

Typography scale, muted color contrast, and simplified cards were refined so the app feels useful and approachable even during stressful routines.
