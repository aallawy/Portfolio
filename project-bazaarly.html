Bazaarly

A same-day delivery app concept built for local stores across the GTA. Bazaarly was designed around the parts of shopping big platforms usually miss: changing inventory, real conversations with retailers, and a service model that supports independent businesses instead of forcing them into chain-store expectations. I developed the UX research, wireframes, case-study thinking, logo, mascot, and brand colors so the product could feel both practical and distinctly local.

Project story

Reframing same-day delivery around how local stores actually work.

Bazaarly began with a gap in the current delivery landscape. Large platforms like DoorDash and Instacart are usually structured around big chains, stable stock, and standardized store systems. That model leaves very little room for neighborhood retailers whose inventory changes quickly, whose products are often more specific, and whose customers still need direct reassurance before they buy.

Using primary and secondary research, including interviews with both consumers and retailers, I shaped Bazaarly as a more human local-commerce experience. The goal was to bring the real-world feeling of browsing different GTA stores onto a phone without pretending that every item will always be available. Instead of hiding that reality, the UX embraces it and builds trust around it.

Research and process

Grounding the app in real shopper behavior and retailer constraints.

The research showed two things at once: shoppers want the speed and convenience of delivery, but local retailers need a system that respects changing stock, direct questions, and the way neighborhood stores actually sell. Bazaarly was designed in response to both sides of that tension.

01

Interviewing both sides of the marketplace

I used interviews with consumers and retailers, alongside secondary research, to identify the trust gaps, service frustrations, and inventory mismatches that happen when local stores are forced into chain-based delivery systems.

02

Designing for flexible availability

Instead of treating every out-of-stock moment like a UX failure, Bazaarly frames limited availability as part of normal local shopping behavior. That decision helped the app feel more honest and more aligned with in-person browsing.

03

Building trust through smarter support

The direct retailer chat and built-in AI assistant work together so users can ask about stock before ordering, compare options across stores, and make more confident decisions without leaving the app.

Experience direction

A delivery app that behaves more like local shopping than instant fulfillment.

Bazaarly was designed to preserve the feeling of discovering something across different neighborhood stores rather than flattening everything into a generic feed. The experience lets users browse, compare, ask questions, and understand that some products may shift in and out of availability just like they would in real life.

That change in framing matters. It makes the service feel more believable, reduces frustration, and gives local retailers room to participate in digital commerce without pretending they operate like major chains with fixed stock and rigid logistics.

Core product decisions

What makes Bazaarly different

  • Customers can message retailers directly before placing an order, which supports better decisions and clearer expectations
  • Retailers are supported by a flat-fee pricing model instead of percentage-based cuts that can be harder on smaller businesses
  • Product unavailability is framed as a normal part of shopping locally, which makes the UX feel more transparent and less punishing
  • An AI assistant can pull and compare relevant options from different local stores so users can browse more efficiently across the city
Brand identity

A softer local-commerce identity built around friendliness, trust, and character.

The Bazaarly visual system balances calm neutrals with dusty blue surfaces and warmer orange accents so the app feels welcoming rather than corporate. I used that palette to support the local, exploratory tone of the product while keeping the interface clean enough for fast decision-making.

The raccoon mascot helps give the brand more personality and memorability, while the logo and supporting applications extend Bazaarly beyond the interface itself. Together, the identity makes the product feel like a neighborhood service with a point of view instead of another anonymous delivery platform.
